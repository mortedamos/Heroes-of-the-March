# Heroes of the March

v0.3 · Rules and Designer's Notes

# Players Guide

## The Story

For a thousand years the Marchstone held the Long Accord. Five crowns swore peace over that black-veined stone, and it held them to it. No army could take the Marches, and every midsummer orc and dwarf, elf and human traded at the same fair.

This season, just after the last Midsummer Fair, the Marchstone split.

With the stone broken, the old wardhouses across the Marches are failing, and everything the Accord sealed away is walking free. The dead are rising out of the barrows. The Ironbound are marching out of their tombs. Worst of all, Skarra Ironjaw is loose and delighted about it.

Heroes from all five kingdoms ride out, each certain that they alone can make the Marches safe. The stone's closing clause says what happens next:

> *"Should this stone fail, let the Marches pass to the crown that **holds** them."*

The hero who holds the most of the Marches proves their crown the stone's true heir.

## Gameplay

A HERO, aided by COMPANIONS, uses RESOURCES to beat an ENCOUNTER at a LOCATION. Each hero can bid resources against a challenge set by the encounter. The highest total wins the encounter, claims the location, and adds its Renown to their score. The first player to reach **20 Renown** wins the game.

## Types of Cards

During setup the deck is broken into a stack for each type of card.

### Location

Locations are the places across the Marches, and beyond them, where the heroes fight to hold the land. Each location has a **Renown** value and may have a condition that changes the rules while it's in play. Each location is paired with an encounter. The player who beats the encounter most spectacularly claims the location and adds its Renown to their total.

Locations belong to one or more groups: **Realm**, **Capital**, **Accord**, **Wardhouse**, **Otherworld**, **Marchguard** and **Collegium**. Some cards care about these groups.

### Hero

Heroes are the players' avatars. Each hero has three stats, **Physical**, **Mental** and **Guile** (P, M and G), plus an ability and a trigger. Heroes use their stats to win encounters and claim Renown.

- A hero's **ability** is theirs to use as the card describes.
- A hero's **trigger** ("When a 6 is rolled, draw a resource") fires whenever its condition happens, on any player's turn.
- Every hero belongs to a kingdom (Human, Elf, Dwarf, Orc or Halfellow). Most are also **Wardens**, sworn to the old Accord.

### Companions

Companions are put into play during the companion phase. They sit next to the hero card and add their own stats to the total. For example, a player whose hero has P3, M3 and G3 puts a companion with P2, M5 and G2 into play. The player's totals are now P5, M8 and G5.

A hero can normally have at most **two companions** at a time. If a new companion comes into play when two are already in play, it replaces one of them. A companion's ability can be used only once per turn. Companions stay in play from turn to turn until a card removes them or they're replaced in the companion phase.

### Resources

Resources are used to beat encounters. They're what players bid. Some resources add to your total; others subtract from, or change, someone else's.

Two resource tags matter to other cards:
- **Wand:** the everyday magic of the Marches. Some cards boost Wands; others shut them down.
- **Council:** calls other heroes to your side for one encounter.

### Encounter

An encounter is the challenge a player must overcome, usually a villain or a monster loosed from a failing wardhouse. Every encounter lists three challenges, one for each stat. Each challenge gives a die range and a difficulty. For example, the **Basilisk** has a challenge of 13 Physical on a roll of 1–3, written **P13**. A player whose total in the relevant stat is equal to or higher than the difficulty has **survived** the encounter.

Once an encounter is on the table, every player may try to overcome it. First add up the relevant stat from your hero and companions. Then bid resources to help yourself, hinder others, or change the encounter.

## Game Setup

1. Break the deck into four stacks: **hero**, **encounter**, **location** and **resource**. Keep the **companion** stack separate as well.
2. Shuffle every stack and place it face down on the table.
3. Each player draws a hero and places it face up in front of them. This is the hero carrying their banner.
4. Each player draws two companions and puts them into play.
5. Deal each player a number of resource cards equal to the number of players. This number is the **draw size**.
6. Decide who goes first.
7. Play proceeds clockwise.

## The Turn

1. **Beginning of the turn.** The current player draws resource cards until their hand is at least the draw size. There is no maximum hand size.
2. **Companion phase.** Every player may add new companions or discard existing ones. Draw a companion card; you may play it. If you now have more companions than allowed, discard one. You may also discard any companion at any time.
3. **Location phase.** The current player turns over the top location card. This is where the encounter takes place.
4. **Encounter phase.** The current player turns over the top encounter card.
   1. If the encounter says so, draw more encounter cards as **minions**. Each minion adds its minion value to the encounter's challenge.
      1. If a card put into play as a minion would draw a minion of its own, it doesn't. Minions may not have minions.
      2. If a particular type of minion must be drawn and there are none left in the stack, shuffle the discard stack and search it instead.
   2. Roll a die to choose which of the encounter's challenges is faced, unless a card lets a player choose.
5. **Bidding phase.** Players bid resources until everyone passes.
   1. Each player either passes or places one resource card in front of them.
      1. A player's first bid is face up.
      2. Further bids are face down.
   2. Bidding goes clockwise.
   3. Bidding ends when every player has passed.
6. **Determine the outcome.**
   1. Reveal face-down bids one at a time, starting with the player who would have bid next. A resource's effect happens as soon as it's revealed.
   2. Total each player's relevant stat: hero, companions in play, and resources bid.
   3. There are three outcomes:
      1. **Loss:** a player whose total is lower than the challenge's difficulty has been beaten. **Their hero falls.**
      2. **Survival:** a player whose total is equal to or higher than the difficulty has survived.
      3. **Winning:** surviving players compare totals. The highest total wins the location and its Renown.
7. **End of the turn.**
   1. Discard the encounter card.
   2. If no one claimed the location, discard it.
   3. If a player has reached 20 Renown, they win. Otherwise the next player clockwise takes their turn.

## Special Phases

### Bidding

Starting with the current player and going clockwise, each player may engage the encounter by bidding a resource card from their hand, placing it on the table in front of them. A player's first card is face up; any further cards are face down. When every player has passed, all bids are placed. Face-down cards are then turned up one at a time, clockwise, starting with the last player to pass. Resource effects happen as soon as a card is turned face up, and all bonuses are totalled.

Continuing the example above: a player whose total Physical (hero, companions and resources bid) is 13 or higher beats the Basilisk's P13 challenge.

At the end of bidding:

1. A player who can't beat the challenge has failed the encounter. **Their hero falls.**
2. Players whose total in the relevant stat beats the difficulty have survived.
3. Surviving players compare their totals. The highest total wins the location and adds it to their winnings. Break ties with a die roll. Abilities and triggers set off by die rolls don't fire on a tiebreak roll.

Encounters that draw minions add each minion's value to the first encounter's challenge.

At the end of each encounter, discard it and every card that was bid.

### Falling (Passing the Banner)

When a player fails to survive an encounter, their hero **falls**: they're carried from the field and someone else takes up the banner. A hero only falls on their own player's turn. If you fail an encounter on someone else's turn, wait. At the start of your turn, shuffle your hero back into the hero stack, draw a new hero, and put them into play at once. Companions and the resources in your hand are not affected by a fall and are not discarded.

# Designers Guide

## The Math

For each individual stat:

- Companion stats range 1–6, a midpoint of 3.5.
- Hero stats range 1–12, a midpoint of 6.5.
- With one companion, 2–18 (midpoint 10).
- With two companions, 3–24 (midpoint 13.5).

Each resource should add 3.5, *if* it adds. Resources equally add or subtract. If so, non-companion resources can't be counted mathematically when considering beating an encounter.

Two things to consider: beating an encounter, and beating an opponent. If resource cards are balanced against each other, beating an opponent should simply come down to spending more cards than they do.

| Difficulty | Situation | Difficulty number |
|---|---|---|
| Trivial | Hero alone | 6.5 |
| Easy | Hero + 1 companion | 10 |
| Normal | Hero + 2 companions | 13.5 |
| Difficult | Hero + 3 resources | 17 |
| Very difficult | Hero + 4 resources | 20.5 |

**Winning condition.** In a three-player game, a game should last about five rounds. So the Renown needed to win should be 5 × the average location value. With location values from 1 to 6 that's 17.5; round it to **20** and skew the average slightly toward 4.

## Ability Ideas

1. On your turn you may use your hero's mental stat in place of another stat.
2. If the current encounter challenge would be decided by a die roll, you may select which challenge to face instead.
3. On your turn you may force another player to use the physical stat of one of their companions in place of another stat.
4. On your turn you may force another player to use the mental stat of one of their companions in place of another stat.
5. On your turn you may force another player to use the guile stat of one of their companions in place of another stat.
6. On any turn, at the end of bidding after all cards have been revealed, you may roll a die once. On a 3 or higher you gain 3 mental. On a 2 or lower you lose 4 mental.
7. +1 to all stats for each hero you have in play.
8. +1 to all stats for each companion you have in play.
9. +1 to all stats for every Marchguard companion in play.
10. +1 to all stats for every Goose companion in play.
11. +1 to all stats for every companion of your hero's kingdom you have in play.
12. During bidding you may shuffle the current location back into the location stack and draw a new location.
13. On your turn you may look at the next two locations. You may replace the current location with one of them. Shuffle the other two back into the location stack.
14. Once per turn you may re-use a once-per-turn ability of a companion or hero.
15. Once per turn you may counter or disable one ability of another player's heroes or companions.
16. Roll a die. On a 4 or higher the current encounter challenge gains no bonuses from minions.
17. If the current encounter challenge would be decided by another player, you may instead roll a die and use that challenge.
18. If the current encounter challenge would be decided by a die roll, you may select which challenge to face instead.
19. During bidding, roll a die. On a 4 or higher you may discard the current encounter and any minions in play and draw a new encounter instead.
20. At the end of bidding, roll a die. On a 4 or higher, exchange control of one of your companions with another player's companion. On a 3 or lower, discard one of your companions.
21. At the start of your turn, before the location or encounter is played, choose an opponent. That opponent takes −3 to a stat of your choice until the end of the turn.
22. At the start of your turn, choose an opponent. That opponent can't gain bonuses from, or use the abilities of, a companion of your choice until the end of the turn.
23. During bidding you may roll a die. On a 4 or higher you may take control of a minion in play and gain its minion value as a bonus to a stat of your choice. On a 3 or lower the current encounter's difficulty is increased by 3 for you.
24. Once per turn you may roll a die. On a 4 or higher, force an opponent to discard a companion. On a 3 or lower, discard one of your own companions. You may use this ability only while you have a companion in play.

## Parts of an Encounter Card

1. **Name.** The name of the encounter.
2. **Picture.** A picture of the encounter.
3. **Group.** The villain group the encounter belongs to. Some resources and abilities use it to modify the encounter. The groups are Undead, Necromancer, Ironbound, Wicker, Oathbreaker, Unremembered, Gargoyle, Wyrmkin, Fey Court, Mossfolk, Warborn, Frostborn, Changeling, Construct, Phantom and Spider, plus Human and Elf where a villain also belongs to a kingdom. If more than one group is listed, all apply.
4. **Minion value.** If this card is put on the table as a minion, this value is added to the main encounter's challenge difficulty, whichever stat that challenge uses.
5. **Quote (optional).** A line of flavor text.
6. **Conditions.** Special conditions that apply to the encounter, even when the card is in play as a minion. Examples:
   1. Humans take −1 to all stats.
   2. When this card comes into play, search the encounter deck for an Undead card, put it into play as a minion, then shuffle the encounter deck.
   3. Wands give no bonuses against this encounter.
   4. When this card comes into play, draw another location and put it into play. The winner takes both locations.
   5. This card can't be played as a minion. If it's drawn as a minion, draw another encounter card and shuffle this one back into the encounter deck.
7. **Challenge determination.** Either the current player chooses or a die roll decides.
8. **Challenges.**
   1. **Frequency.** If the challenge is decided by a die roll, the die range that selects it.
   2. **Relevant stat.** The stat used to decide whether the challenge is overcome.
   3. **Difficulty number.** How hard the challenge is.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Renown | A location's point value. The first player to 20 Renown wins. |
| Fall | A beaten hero leaves play at the start of their player's next turn, and a new hero takes up the banner. |
| Warden | A hero or companion sworn to the old Accord. Most heroes are Wardens. |
| Oathbreaker | A Warden who broke their oath: the villains of the Accord's own ranks. |
| Marchguard | The Accord's joint peacekeeping force, drawn from all five kingdoms. |
| Collegium | Westmarch's college of magi, keepers of dangerous relics. |

| Shard of the Marchstone | Six resource cards that reward collecting them together. |

## Changes in v0.3

- **Reskinned** to the Kingdom Marches setting. Every stat, value, die range and difficulty from the stat sheet is unchanged.
- **One version per character.** Every hero and companion appears once. Four new companions replace the second versions: Tova Emberdeep, Tansy Brambleby, Sir Osric Vane and Elder Ilvena. The four Geese are now four different geese.
- **Thirteen cards restored** from the earlier printed card sheets: 2 companions, 3 locations, 7 encounters and 1 resource. Their printed difficulties are converted to the stat sheet's scale (15 → 13, 12 → 11, 10 → 9, 8 → 7, 5 → 4).
- **Hero triggers** (the "draw a resource when…" line) are now printed on every hero card.
- **Blank abilities filled** from the earlier printed card sheets, plus Ability Ideas 13 and 19 and one new Council card (The Gathering of Heroes).
- **Location conditions filled** where the stat sheet was blank.
- **New group tags:** Underway (powers Varg Ironjaw), Goose & Kettle (powers the Goose & Kettle location), Spider.
